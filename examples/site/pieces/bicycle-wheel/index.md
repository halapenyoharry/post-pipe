An old steel bicycle asks for very little, provided its moving parts are kept clean and true. When a rim begins to rub against the brake pad on every turn, a quarter-turn of the spoke wrench brings it back into line.

Patience matters more than force. Tightening one spoke on the right while loosening its neighbor on the left pulls the rim a fraction of a millimeter without throwing the wheel out of round.

With fresh grease in the bearings and a drop of light oil on each chain link, the bicycle rolls down the gravel lane in near silence, save for the steady ticking of the rear hub.
