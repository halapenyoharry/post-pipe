Where the footpath leaves the gravel road and drops into the alder grove, the river makes a wide, slow turn over flat limestone shelves. In early spring the water runs high and amber, carrying twigs and pine needles from the hills upstream.

By June the current settles into a clear channel along the far bank, leaving shallow pools warm enough for minnows on the inside of the bend. Flat stones along the edge dry quickly in the morning wind.

Anyone walking the bank can read the week's weather in the waterline on the bridge piers and the drift of sand gathered behind the roots of the old willow trees.
