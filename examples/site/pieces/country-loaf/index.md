A good loaf needs very little attention if given enough time. Flour, warm water, a pinch of salt, and a spoonful of starter come together in a wide ceramic bowl before supper, forming a rough, shaggy mass.

Three folds over the first two hours give the dough its strength. After that, the covered bowl rests on the cool pantry counter overnight while the kitchen sleeps.

In the morning the dough has doubled, domed and blistered at the edges. Baked inside a heavy iron pot, the crust turns a deep chestnut brown and crackles softly as it cools on the wire rack.
