The soil at the north end of the yard stays cold well into March, holding the winter damp under a thin crust of dead leaves. Once the ground yields to a spade without clumping, the first beds can be turned and raked level.

Spinach and peas go in first along the low wooden fence. They do not mind a light frost at night so long as the afternoon sun reaches the raised earth for a few hours.

By mid-April, thin green rows mark where the seeds were dropped by hand. A wooden barrel at the corner of the shed catches rain from the tin roof, keeping water close for the dry weeks ahead.
